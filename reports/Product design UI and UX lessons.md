# Subtract, slow down, and let the street teach

The experts mostly agree on one message for Arabic World: **teach through the world rather than through text, cut every element that is not the street, the word or the errand, and make the player more capable instead of making the app more sticky.** Miyamoto's World 1-1, Ueda's "design by subtraction", Jenova Chen's HUD-less Journey, Unpacking's no-fail object storytelling and Chants of Sennaar's "see every word at least twice" rule all point to the same design. One errand at a time, one obvious action, safe mistakes, and growth shown as "what you can now say", not as points. Product-design and accessibility sources turn this into numbers. **Tap targets of 48 CSS px with 8 px gaps** (WCAG AA floor 24 px), **contrast of 4.5:1 or more**, self-paced text, and reduced-motion support. The Arabic typography evidence is thin but consistent. Fully vowelled Naskh text needs **at least 18 px (start at 20–22 px)** and a **line-height of about 2.0**, and must never sit in a clipped box. Learners need far slower reading than Netflix Arabic's 17–20 characters per second. The language-app evidence supports the project's no-streak stance: peer-reviewed work finds broken streaks make users more likely to quit. It also shows the one Duolingo lesson worth copying, which is letting people play before any sign-up. The biggest risks the sources flag are a calm screen that hides the current goal (Hodent), a gentle first errand that feels like "only a tutorial" (Portal), and speech bubbles that clip tashkeel or run off-screen. The cheapest fix for all three is a $0 RITE-style playtest: 3–5 people, a silent observer, a fix and a retest. Many specific numbers below come from secondary sources, and each is marked **[unverified]** where so.

## Five legends converge on one rule: the street is the tutorial

Nintendo's method is the clearest template for a wordless errand. Miyamoto describes World 1-1 as containing everything a player needs to "gradually and naturally understand" the game. Its layout teaches survivable mistakes: some pits have floors ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-miyamoto-built-i-super-mario-bros-i-legendary-world-1-1); [Wikipedia, World 1-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_1-1)). Koichi Hayashida built Super Mario 3D Land levels on **kishotenketsu, a four-act form: introduce, develop, twist, conclude**, with no antagonist required ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2012/04/super_mario_3d_land_director_shares_level_design_inspirations); [MCV on GMTK](https://www.mcvuk.com/development/video-nintendos-level-design-secrets-in-four-steps)). Each 3D Land level is built around one mechanic ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/lessons-from-suzy-cube-level-design-breakdown)). For Arabic World this maps one-to-one onto an errand. The baker hands you bread as you hear "khubz" (introduce). You then ask for two loaves (develop). The same root reappears on a sign (twist). The errand closes with a nod and the item in your basket (conclude).

Fumito Ueda gives the editing rule. He "didn't hold back on removing and subtracting elements," shipped Ico with no stats or scoring, and left the key jump in the game unprompted ([Shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/ico/)). Jenova Chen's Journey removed the HUD, the lobby and visible names; the only social tool is a chirp ([MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/)). His thesis argues that **players should set their own difficulty through the core actions**, not through menus ([Flow in Games](https://jenovachen.info/abstract)). Journey's robed, faceless figure is the closest shipped precedent for Arabic World's hero, and it shows that posture, gesture and cloth movement can carry emotion. In Arabic World, the difficulty choice becomes which stall to approach next, and the "chirp" becomes a non-lexical greeting gesture such as a hand to the chest. Both are design inferences, not quoted practice.

Cozy designers add the emotional brief. Unpacking's pillars are **contemplation, discovery and expression, with no scores, no fail states and no negative consequences**. Its story is told entirely through objects, and the team cut features to get there ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/unpacking-the-design-pillars-of-a-chill-puzzle-game); [GDC 2022 coverage](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/gdc-2022-unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items)). The Project Horseshoe 2017 report defines coziness as **safety, abundance and softness** **[unverified in primary text]** ([Pixelpoppers link](https://pixelpoppers.com/link/d64e95/group-report-coziness-in-games-an-exploration-of-safety-softness-and-satisfied-needs)). A Short Hike began as a small side prototype with free routing across one dense island ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-a-short-hike-postmortem)). That model fits a $0 single-quarter build. Firewatch's Sean Vanaman adds that writing should be short, with natural break points, and should either stay out of gameplay or be the gameplay ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/why-game-design-should-feel-like-taking-your-player-out-on-a-date)).

The theory underneath is Raph Koster's: fun comes from mastering patterns, and boredom sets in when there are no patterns left to absorb or the pattern is out of reach ([Koster PDF](https://www.raphkoster.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Theory-of-Fun-10-Years-Later.pdf)). Language is pattern learning, so Arabic roots and plural patterns are the "levels". Will Wright adds two cautions. Players always build a mental model, and **if they cannot see what caused an event, it feels random**. Models with less detail are also easier for people to work with ([Koster's notes on Wright](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=334)). In practice, when the player brings the wrong item, show why: the label and the word. Keep each scene's object count low enough that its vocabulary stays learnable.

The language-puzzle games supply the most directly usable rules. Chants of Sennaar requires **every word to appear "at least twice" in different situations**. It uses the number of unresolved words as its difficulty lever. When testers struggle, the team adds contexts rather than simplifying rules ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/immersing-players-in-the-culture-of-a-people-with-language-puzzler-chants-of-sennaar)). Heaven's Vault lets players mistranslate without being told; Jon Ingold notes that games are usually too cautious about such ambiguity ([GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/archives/327504-heaven-s-vault-devs-on-the-importance-of-letting-players-get-things-wrong)). Obra Dinn confirms deductions only in batches of three, which discourages guessing. That rationale is critics' reading, not Lucas Pope's own words ([Film Stories](https://filmstories.co.uk/?p=83249)). Animal Crossing ties play to a real clock, so big changes take a day and sessions stay short ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/why-nintendo-s-new-i-animal-crossing-i-features-allow-instant-change-in-the-real-time-game)). Arabic World's fixed 08:00–11:00 window works better as "one morning per visit" with small fresh details each time, such as a new stall item or a neighbour's greeting. It should avoid Animal Crossing's admitted "inconvenience" pull ([Game Developer GDC coverage](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-is-that-a-franchise-in-your-pocket-an-i-animal-crossing-wild-world-i-case-study)).

## Product designers set the numbers: 48 px targets, one action, honest feedback

Dieter Rams' principles reduce to **useful, understandable, unobtrusive, honest, thorough, and as little design as possible**. "Honest" means design that "makes no promises it can't keep" ([Vitsoe](https://www.vitsoe.com/gb/about/good-design)). That is a direct argument against fake progress fanfare. Don Norman's discoverability concepts (affordances, signifiers, constraints, mappings, feedback, conceptual model) become concrete here. Put the Arabic word on or beside the object it names (mapping). Glow only the tappable thing (signifier). Show 2–4 plausible choices, never a full list (constraint) ([Actionable Books summary](https://www.actionablebooks.com/summaries/the-design-of-everyday-things)). Nielsen's ten heuristics give the audit list. The most relevant are **recognition rather than recall** (keep picture and word visible together early), **user control and freedom** (always a way back or out), and **help users recover from errors** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)). Steve Krug's rule is "self-evident, then self-explanatory". Cut "happy talk" and instructions nobody reads ([summary](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/dont-make-me-think/)).

Bret Victor and Alan Kay give the learning ladder. Victor says an environment should explain itself and show behaviour rather than make learners simulate it in their heads ([Learnable Programming](https://worrydream.com/LearnableProgramming)). Kay's "doing with images makes symbols" (enactive, then iconic, then symbolic) ([Kay slides](https://www.softwareresearch.net/fileadmin/user_upload/Teaching/WS16/SE_Alan_Kay.Doing_with_images_makes_symbols.pdf)) suggests this order: **do** (pick up the bread), **see** (picture plus word), **read** (vowelled script), and only then **type**. That matches Arabic World's three reply modes: Musāfir (tap/act), Ṭālib (tiles) and Muqīm (keyboard). Kathy Sierra asks designers to make users capable rather than make the product loved. She is reported to call typical gamification "another form of Skinner" (reviewer paraphrase) ([mtlynch](https://mtlynch.io/book-reports/badass/)). John Maeda's "subtract the obvious, add the meaningful" ([fs.blog](https://fs.blog/the-laws-of-simplicity/)) points the same way. The no-XP rule only works if something real replaces the score: a personal list of "things I can now say in the city".

Calm technology is the closest single brief to this project. Weiser and Brown describe technology that moves between the centre and periphery of attention, with the person in control of that move: "offer, but not demand" ([Calm Technology](https://calmtech.com/papers/designing-calm-technology)). Amber Case's principles add **"smallest possible amount of attention"** and **"work even when it fails"** ([calmtech.com](https://calmtech.com/)). In Arabic World the periphery is the morning light, the ambient sound and a soft glow on the next errand object. Details arrive only when the player taps. If audio fails, the text and picture still carry the line. Brignull's dark-pattern catalogue names what to ban outright: nagging, fake urgency, confirmshaming, preselection and addictive design ([deceptive.design](https://www.deceptive.design/types)). A 2026 ACM IDC paper on Duolingo describes **"exit dark patterns"**, **"goal drift"** (learning turns into keeping metrics) and **"gamified obligation"** (guilt on leaving) (secondhand; [deceptive.design](https://deceptive.design/articles/the-evil-bird-and-the-right-to-disconnect-children-s-vulnerability-to-exit-dark-patterns-in-gamified-educational-apps)). Arabic World's answer is to make quitting silent and easy, and to resume exactly where the player stopped.

The hard numbers for touch and contrast are well sourced. **WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 (AA) requires 24×24 CSS px targets, and SC 2.5.5 (AAA) asks for 44×44** ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). **Material specifies 48×48 dp with 8 dp or more between targets** ([Android accessibility](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)). Apple's 44 pt minimum comes from mirrored HIG copies **[unverified against live page]**. Steven Hoober finds that targets at the top and bottom edges need **10–12 mm** spacing versus about 7 mm in the centre. He also finds that a docked bottom bar works only with **four or fewer** items ([UXmatters](http://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/11/design-for-fingers-and-thumbs-instead-of-touch.php)). Contrast floors are **4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and UI parts, and 7:1 for a high-contrast mode** ([Unity accessibility basics](https://unity.com/blog/video-game-accessibility-basics)). NN/g suggests using about three distinct text sizes and never lowering contrast as a way to de-emphasise something ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/)). The awkward consequence is that a 28+-key Arabic keyboard cannot reach 44 px per key in portrait on a typical phone. It needs taller keys, a split or paged layout, or tiles first.

Celia Hodent ties these together for games. Her framework splits UX into **usability and "engage-ability"**, and her seven usability pillars are adapted from Nielsen ([uxdesign.cc](https://uxdesign.cc/ux-and-video-game-design-5d8bcc50be67)). Good signs and feedback reduce the need for tutorial text. She also warns that **removing HUD elements for diegetic purity can raise cognitive load and cause frustration** ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/features/how-neuroscience-and-ux-impacts-video-game-design)). This is the essential counterweight to Ueda and Chen. A calm screen still has to show the current errand and how to get a hint. Her motivation model uses competence, autonomy and relatedness ([UX Podcast](https://uxpod.com/episodes/competence-autonomy-relatedness-celia-hodent-on-games-design.html)). The exact wording of the seven pillars was not retrieved **[unverified]**.

## Bubbles and vowelled Arabic need room: 20 px type, line-height 2, slow reading

No source documents Arabic-comics balloon conventions, so the bubble rules below combine Western lettering practice with RTL inference. Blambot's lettering grammar says to **aim the tail at the speaker's mouth** and end it **about 50–60% of the way from balloon to head**. When two speakers share a panel, stagger and connect their balloons. Avoid "tangents", where a balloon nearly touches another element ([Blambot](https://blambot.com/articles_grammar.shtml); [Tapas](https://tapas.io/newsfeed/183)). Faceless characters have no mouth, so the tail should aim at a fixed "voice point" at the top-centre of the head. Manga, which also reads right to left, orders balloons right to left and top to bottom ([Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/EAWB4YvyVDqJJQ)). If two bubbles are ever on screen together, the earlier line belongs to the right and higher (inference). The safer rule is **one speaker's bubble at a time**. The Game Accessibility Guidelines require a **visual indication of who is speaking** and **text that advances at the player's own pace** ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)). With no portrait or face, a short name tag next to the tail does that job. Oxenfree is the one well-documented shipped case of world-anchored bubbles. It used long camera shots so the player, NPCs and bubbles all stayed in frame ([Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-17-creating-narrative-gameplay-oxenfree/)). Its designer later said the studio did not want to be known for bubbles over heads ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-night-school-studio-s-i-oxenfree-i-)). Animal Crossing instead keeps a fixed bottom text box and gives each character its own per-letter sound ([Game Revolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/guides/640242-animal-crossing-new-horizons-speed-up-text-conversations-speech)). Under the no-music rule, such sounds must be plain clicks or left out.

The technical approach is clear from the drei docs and forum reports. `<Html>` in its default non-transform mode keeps text crisp. `calculatePosition` is the hook for clamping the bubble to the screen ([drei docs](https://drei.docs.pmnd.rs/misc/html)). The built-in `occlude` prop has reported performance costs ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/html-tag-low-performance-react/48917)), and `occlude="blending"` can hide HTML under post-processing ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/react-three-drei-htmls-occlude-blending-prop-hides-html-leva-etc-when-post-processing-pass-is-used/74487)). Since the street uses post-processing, avoid both. Instead, project the head point each frame, clamp the bubble inside a safe-area inset, and re-aim the tail at the real speaker. When a bubble is pushed on-screen, the tail must move with it ([Adventure Creator forum](https://adventurecreator.org/forum/discussion/4384/dialogue-flickers-when-character-close-to-edge-of-screen)). Off-screen speakers get an edge arrow ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/putting-an-indicator-on-the-edge-of-the-screen-for-an-off-screen-target/29020)). Any performance claim for this approach on phones is **[unverified]**. No benchmark was found.

Typography is where the evidence is thinnest and the risk highest. The W3C Arabic Layout Requirements note that Arabic ascenders and descenders reach much further than Latin ones. Fonts also place vowel marks, and shadda with kasra, differently ([W3C ALReq](https://www.w3.org/TR/alreq/)). A Google fonts thread advises always setting `line-height` for Arabic, because many fonts place marks outside the line box. A LibreOffice bug shows Amiri's tashkeel cropped when line spacing is tight ([Google Groups](https://groups.google.com/g/early-access-fonts/c/-V9j-XYmWyA); [LibreOffice bug](https://lists.freedesktop.org/archives/libreoffice-bugs/2017-October/048489.html)). The only size study found involved children aged 9–12. It found 10 pt Arabic unreadable and recommended **at least 18** ([Huddersfield](https://pure.hud.ac.uk/en/publications/the-optimum-font-size-and-type-for-students-aged-9-12-reading-ara/)). Combining these gives starting values of **20–22 px Arabic, never below 18 px, line-height about 1.9–2.1, at least 8 px of vertical bubble padding, and `overflow: visible`**. These are **[unverified starting values to test on a real phone]**. The game currently names system fonts (Geeza Pro first). That means vowel placement differs between iPhone and Android, and the safer move is to self-host one OFL Naskh font. Scheherazade New (SIL OFL) supports full diacritics and offers lowered or raised shadda+kasra placement ([SIL](https://software.sil.org/scheherazade/features/)). Amiri is OFL but no longer developed ([GitHub](https://github.com/aliftype/amiri)). The measured Google Fonts Arabic subsets are:

| Font | Arabic subset (woff2, measured 18 Nov 2026) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Scheherazade New 400 | 79 KB | Designed for full tashkeel; shadda placement option |
| Noto Naskh Arabic (variable) | 94 KB | Simpler forms; needs a Latin fallback |
| Amiri 400 | 108 KB | Classical; development ended 2022 |
| Cairo / IBM Plex Sans Arabic | 31–34 KB | Sans; mark stacking untested **[unverified]** |

Because the game's text is a closed set, subsetting to the glyphs actually used, while keeping the `mark`, `mkmk`, `ccmp`, `calt` and `liga` features, should shrink the file well below 79 KB **[unverified]**.

Reading speed is the final constraint. **Netflix Arabic allows at most 42 characters per line, two lines, and 20 characters per second for adults (17 for children)**. It also forbids line breaks between noun and modifier or verb and subject ([Netflix Arabic guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/215517947)). The BBC is cited at 160–180 words per minute **[secondary only]** ([Clevercast](https://clevercast.com/bbc-subtitling-guidelines)). These figures are for fluent readers of mostly unvowelled text. A learner reading full tashkeel needs much less: roughly **8–10 CPS at most** **[unverified judgement]**, which in practice means no timed dismissal at all. A bubble should hold **1–2 lines of about 25–35 characters**. A common typewriter speed of 30–50 ms per character works out to 20–33 CPS ([Abratabia](https://abratabia.com/game-ui-design/dialogue-ui.php)). That is too fast, and a letter-by-letter reveal also breaks Arabic letter joining, so reveal whole words or the whole line. Netflix uses diacritics only where needed. That supports a learner toggle that fades vowel marks later, but it does not justify removing them from teaching lines.

## Language apps teach what to copy (play first, adapt difficulty) and what to refuse (streak guilt)

Duolingo's own growth lead reports that **moving sign-up after a first lesson raised daily active users by about 20%**. Softening the sign-up wall to a quiet "Later" button added **8.2%** (company-reported; [First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker)). Arabic World already goes further, with no account and progress stored on the device, and should keep it that way. Duolingo's Birdbrain predicts whether a learner will get each exercise right and uses that to choose difficulty ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/learning-how-to-help-you-learn-introducing-birdbrain)). The project's Lexical Director already follows this "just hard enough" idea without points. On streaks, Silverman et al. (2023, *Journal of Consumer Research*) found that users can come to prioritise the streak over the activity, that **broken streaks make users more likely to quit**, and that paid streak repair lowers motivation. Read via a summary ([The Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/es-ES/insights/consumer-insights/streak-creep-the-perils-of-too-much-gamification)). Duolingo's own "Streak Freeze" rationale, that slack motivates better than rigid rules, quietly concedes the downside ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). Independent evidence that Duolingo teaches well is limited. Its headline efficacy study was company-funded and had no comparison group ([Vesselinov & Grego PDF](https://rebrand.avantassessment.com/wp-content/uploads/The-effectiveness-of-Duolingo-in-developing-receptive-and-productive-language-knowledge-and-proficiency.pdf)).

Immersion games show the gap Arabic World can fill. Influent is praised for in-context words and native audio but criticised as vocabulary-only, with no grammar or sentence building ([Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/influent)). Noun Town's evidence is mostly marketing plus a small perception pilot ([RISTAL 2025](https://reference-global.com/2/v2/download/article/10.2478/ristal-2025-0008.pdf)). Meta-analyses find a positive overall effect for digital game-based vocabulary learning, with wide variation between studies ([Tsai & Tsai](https://scholar.lib.ntnu.edu.tw/en/publications/digital-game-based-second-language-vocabulary-learning-and-condit-2/); [Cambridge 2021, d=0.64](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/08A549A6CFD1078406E6A4F8AFE28184/S0958344021000239a.pdf/a-meta-analysis-examining-technology-assisted-l2-vocabulary-learning.pdf)). Errands with typed replies are the kind of sentence-level task reviewers say these games lack. Anki's best-known failure is a growing review backlog ([Matuschak](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zyk3ZCEVSB4Zwej3Xd4CJ2g1FynRFTZGJpQN)). Arabic World's morning check of yesterday's words should stay small and capped, so that a missed week never turns into a pile of debt.

Error feedback has real research behind it. Lyster and Saito's meta-analysis of 15 classroom studies (N=827) found that **prompts, which signal the error and let the learner self-correct, beat recasts, which simply give the right form**. Low-proficiency learners benefited more from prompts in one study ([McGill](https://escholarship.mcgill.ca/downloads/6w924g64b)). That supports a three-step hint ladder: "close", then "look here" (the vowel or letter), then the model form. A missing short vowel should count as "almost right" and get one retry. The ladder and the leniency are design inferences, since no study tested them for Arabic. On motivation, a cross-sectional survey of Duolingo users (n=247) found that gamification worked through **competence and relatedness, not autonomy** ([CEUR-WS](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4043/paper14.pdf)). That points to two replacements for XP: a "things I can now do" journal (competence) and neighbours who remember the player (relatedness). No study has tested non-streak return designs **[unverified as effective]**.

## The first sixty seconds and a $0 test loop decide everything else

Mayer's multimedia principles give screen rules with measured effects from lab lessons, not games. **Redundancy d=0.87, spatial contiguity d=0.79, modality d=0.72, coherence d=0.70, segmenting d=0.70, signalling and pre-training d=0.46** (Mayer 2017 via summary; [Zendy](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/jcal.12197); definitions at [Dartmouth](https://services.dartmouth.edu/TDClient/1806/Portal/KB/PrintArticle?ID=171655)). On screen, this means showing only what serves the current word (coherence) and placing the word next to its object (contiguity). Lines should advance at the player's pace (segmenting). The player can meet the names of key objects before the errand (pre-training). A spoken line should not also be fully captioned by default (modality and redundancy). That last point conflicts with the accessibility rule that speech needs subtitles ([GAG basic](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/basic/)), so captions must be a clear toggle. Chen, Kalyuga and Sweller found that simple word-to-object matches favour letting learners try first, while complex material such as script and grammar patterns favours worked examples ([Chen et al. 2015](https://core.ac.uk/download/293753327.pdf)).

For onboarding, Celeste teaches the dash through early level design alone ([Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/silent-tutorials-player-learning)). Portal's testers called the opening a "great tutorial" without realising it was the game ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/thinking-with-portals-creating-valve-s-new-ip)). The lesson cuts both ways: teach silently, but give the first errand a small real goal so it does not feel like a warm-up. Valve's playtest lessons are that **players do not learn well when stressed, and they do not look up** ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/valves-secret-weapon)). The next errand cue should therefore sit at eye level in the street, not in a corner badge. Dan Cook's skill atoms (action, simulation, feedback, synthesis) frame each errand as one learnable loop that later errands build on ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/feature-the-chemistry-of-game-design-)). Industry Day-1 retention benchmarks come from monetised games and do not apply here ([Roblox docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/analytics/retention)). A step funnel still does: scene entered, first word heard, first errand done, second errand started. It shows where people stop, though not why ([GameAnalytics](https://gameanalytics.com/blog/tips-for-a-great-first-time-user-experience-ftue-in-f2p-games)).

The $0 testing method is well established. Microsoft's **RITE** method changes the interface as soon as a problem and a clear fix are seen, even after a single participant, and tests the fix on the next person ([Wikipedia, RITE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RITE_Method)). NN/g's model says **5 users find about 85% of problems, and three rounds of five beat one round of fifteen**. For distinct user groups, test about 3 people per group ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/)). Critics note the model mostly catches common problems, and one reappraisal suggests 27–30 users are needed for fuller coverage ([MeasuringU](https://measuringu.com/five-users/)). Krug's version is one morning a month with three people and a same-day debrief ([Brikman](https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2014/09/26/dont-make-me-think-revisited/)). Think-aloud means the observer stays quiet and does not lead, and gently prompts the tester if they go silent ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/thinking-aloud-the-1-usability-tool/)). Valve observers give no hints, treat body language as more revealing than answers afterwards, and usually ignore testers' proposed fixes ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/valves-secret-weapon)). Full PLAY heuristics (about 50 items) are too long for non-experts to use ([NExPlay 2018](https://scitepress.org/Papers/2018/66956/pdf/index.html)), so a 10-item list is more practical. The project's Playwright lab can catch mechanical faults (target size, overlaps, dead ends, contrast) before any human sees the game. Only people can say whether a line was understood or whether a morning felt calm.

## Conclusion

The research changes one framing. Arabic World's hard rules (no faces, no music, no XP, mornings only) are not restrictions that need to be designed around. They are close to the "subtraction" that Ueda, Chen and the Unpacking team chose freely, and the evidence on streaks and exit dark patterns suggests they protect learning as well as values. The real design risk is the opposite failure. A screen stripped too far hides the goal, a bubble clips its vowels, a keyboard is too small for thumbs, and a first errand feels like a tutorial. Every one of these is measurable: 48 px, 4.5:1, 20 px at line-height 2, ≤35 characters per line, and the first errand completed by 3 of 3 silent testers. That means the lab can enforce the craft side, while a handful of real people on real phones judge the feel.

One local finding needs Edward's attention. `STATE.md` still describes the street as "at dusk", which conflicts with hard rule 5 (world only 08:00–11:00). Cycle 7's clock-driven sun may already have fixed this, but the wording and the scene clocks should be checked.
