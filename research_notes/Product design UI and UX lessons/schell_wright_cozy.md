# Schell, Wright, Hodent, cozy/calm design and Nintendo: lessons for Arabic World

## 1. Jesse Schell, The Art of Game Design: which lenses apply

### Takeaway
The book is organised as 100+ numbered "lenses" (sets of questions); the first is the Lens of Experience. The exact lens texts could not be fetched this round, so the one-line applications below rest on the lens names plus general knowledge of the book, flagged [unverified].

### Cited Findings
- The book presents over 100 sets of questions ("lenses") for viewing a design; Lens #1 is the Lens of Experience (the catalogue calls it "Experience", not "Essential Experience") — [CIRCA summary of Schell](https://circa.cs.ualberta.ca/index.php/CIRCA:The_Art_of_Game_Design_-_Jesse_Schell) (as reported by search snippet; page itself failed to load on fetch); [O'Reilly listing](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/-/9781466598645/)
- Lens #30 is the Lens of Fairness (cited in a book review) — [Museum of Play review](https://www.museumofplay.org/app/uploads/2022/01/3-3-book-review-5.pdf)
- A Lens of Reward exists in the book — [Course page on Schell's lenses](https://johnfjennings.github.io/coursefoundry-ludology-and-game-play-pages/week-05/schells-lenses.html)

### Inferences
Applications to Arabic World, [unverified] against the book text (lens names from the book's table of contents; the one-liners are my own):
- Essential Experience: write one sentence ("a calm morning errand where a word you half-knew becomes useful"), and cut features that do not serve it.
- Surprise: small delight, not shock (an unexpected neighbour greeting, a shop detail), never a fail state.
- Curiosity: pose small questions via the world (a sign, an unfamiliar label) that the player resolves by noticing the root or word.
- Flow: errands sized so challenge matches the learner's current words; no timers.
- Problem Solving: each errand = a gentle puzzle (find the right item/word) with hints available.
- Simplicity/Complexity and Elegance: few verbs (walk, look, speak, pick), rich outcomes; each system should do several jobs.
- The Player: model the target player (phone, short sessions, one hand, reads in Arabic script slowly).
- Interest Curve: within a 3-hour in-game morning (08:00-11:00), shape each session as hook, gentle rise, a quiet peak, a calm resolution; avoid constant intensity.
- Fairness and Reward: reward = understanding and warm reactions from neighbours, consistent with "no XP/coins/streaks".

- Verbatim lens questions were not retrievable (CIRCA fetch refused connection; book is paywalled). Confirm each lens number/name against the book before quoting in docs.

## 2. Will Wright: possibility space, mental models, toy before game

### Takeaway
Wright treats a game as a vehicle for exploring a possibility space, argues the designer must help the player build the right mental model (visible cause and effect), and says the best stories are the player's own.

### Cited Findings
- Games should be less like linear media and more like a vehicle for exploring possibility space; "The interesting stories are the player ones." — [Raph Koster notes on Wright's Living Game Worlds 2006 keynote](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=334)
- Toys range from creative (Lego) to representational; models strip irrelevant detail, and "The less detail, the easier it is to edit for people." — [Raph Koster notes](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=334)
- Players always build a mental model; designers must help them build the right one; if players cannot see what caused an event, it feels random — [Raph Koster notes](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=334)
- For The Sims, the team listened to player stories and vocabulary after release; the early dominant word was "dollhouse" — [Raph Koster notes](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=334)
- Wright (2010 talk, via Kotaku reporting) suggests players "mentally demo" a game and that toy play models real things such as traffic — [ISPR summary](https://ispr.info/2010/02/25/will-wright-mental-video-game-playing-and-the-merging-of-virtual-and-real/)
- Spore started closer to a SimEarth-style science toy before becoming a full game (2005 GDC demo notes) — [GameSpot, GDC 07: Creating Spore / GDC 06 coverage](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/gdc-07-creating-spore/1100-6167215/) (search-snippet level only)
- His 2003 GDC talk "Dynamics for Designers" covers simple systems producing large possibility — [Game Developer video page](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-will-wright-s-dynamics-for-designers-from-gdc-2003)

### Inferences
- "Toy before game": ship a pleasant toy first (walk the quarter, tap a thing, hear/see its Arabic word) before any errand structure; if the toy is not pleasant, errands will not rescue it.
- Show causality: when the player brings the wrong item, show why (label, root) so the model forms; never make results feel random.
- Everyday-life emergence: a few shops x a few errands x a few neighbours gives combinatorial variety; let players tell their own "morning" stories.
- Keep models low-detail (fewer, clearer objects) so the vocabulary load is editable and learnable.

### Gaps
- SimCity-specific design lessons were not found in fetched sources; the Sims "emergent everyday life" point is only supported by the dollhouse/stories note. No primary transcript of the Wright talks fetched.

## 3. Celia Hodent's game UX pillars

### Takeaway
Hodent's framework (The Gamer's Brain) has two main UX pillars: usability and "engage-ability". Her seven usability pillars are adapted from Nielsen's 1994 heuristics. I confirmed the structure and a few pillars' gist; exact definitions were not retrievable.

### Cited Findings
- Seven Usability Pillars for Game UX, adapted from Nielsen's 1994 guidelines; the two main UX pillars are usability and engage-ability — [UX Design: Intro to UX in Game Design](https://uxdesign.cc/ux-and-video-game-design-5d8bcc50be67) (cited via search snippet; direct fetch returned 403); [Creative Bloq interview](https://www.creativebloq.com/features/how-neuroscience-and-ux-impacts-video-game-design)
- Signs invite the player to take an action; feedback affirms the action and teaches for the future; good signs and feedback reduce tutorial text, which Hodent thinks should be avoided where possible — [UX Design: Intro to UX in Game Design](https://uxdesign.cc/ux-and-video-game-design-5d8bcc50be67)
- Minimum workload: the brain seeks minimum effort and players use heuristics to "go straight to the point" — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/features/how-neuroscience-and-ux-impacts-video-game-design)
- A Ubisoft UX designer's derived set lists consistency/standards, error prevention and recovery, accessibility/flexibility (derived partly from Hodent, not her own list) — [UX Design: Usability heuristics in games](https://uxdesign.cc/usability-heuristics-and-competition-in-games-707cac36ff12)
- Her motivation framing uses competence, autonomy, relatedness (self-determination theory) — [UX Pod episode](https://uxpod.com/episodes/competence-autonomy-relatedness-celia-hodent-on-games-design.html)

### Inferences
Definitions below are from my recollection of the book, [unverified]; check against The Gamer's Brain before citing:
- Signs and feedback: every actionable thing looks actionable (affordance); every action gets immediate, clear response (soft chime-free visual/ambient cue, since no music).
- Clarity: the player always knows what to do now and what the interface means; one primary action per screen.
- Form follows function: visual form tells you what a thing does; decoration never hides interactables.
- Consistency: same icon/gesture means the same thing everywhere (taps, swipes, word cards).
- Minimum workload: reduce memory and effort; relevant here, keep Arabic script reading load per screen low; offer transliteration/translation on demand.
- Error prevention and recovery: make wrong moves hard to do by accident (big tap targets) and trivially undoable; fits "no fail states".
- Flexibility: support different skill levels and input (one hand, reduced motion, text size).
- Engage-ability: motivation (competence/autonomy/relatedness, with no XP or streaks), emotion (calm, warmth, kindness), game flow (challenge balanced to skill, clear goals, instant feedback).

### Gaps
- Official wording of the seven definitions and of the engage-ability pillars not retrieved (book paywalled; two article fetches 403). Treat as unverified.

## 4. Cozy / calm design

### Takeaway
Cozy design is defined by safety, abundance and softness (Project Horseshoe 2017); calm games remove fail states and pressure, give players self-chosen pace, and make kindness the core verb (Spiritfarer's hugs, Unpacking's quiet storytelling).

### Cited Findings
- Project Horseshoe 2017 report "Coziness in Games: An Exploration of Safety, Softness, and Satisfied Needs" defines coziness through safety, abundance and softness; cozy games do not expect players to struggle for basic needs like food or shelter; higher-order needs include connecting with others — [Search summary of the report](https://pixelpoppers.com/link/d64e95/group-report-coziness-in-games-an-exploration-of-safety-softness-and-satisfied-needs) (page itself only linked the report; details come from search snippet) [unverified in primary text]; [Guardian-style overview syndicated](https://www.newssniffer.co.uk/articles/2809043/versions/16301530)
- Wholesome Games began as a Twitter account by Matthew Taylor (reported as 2019) — [NME](https://www.nme.com/features/wholesome-games-are-for-you-and-theyre-here-to-stay-3014776) (snippet-level); [Engaged Family Gaming](https://engagedfamilygaming.com/wholesome-games-and-the-evolution-of-cozy-games/)
- Unpacking (Witch Beam): three pillars - contemplation, discovery, expression; no scores, no fail states, no negative consequences; no dialogue, story told through belongings placed in rooms; subtractive design (removing features) — [Game Developer: Unpacking the design pillars](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/unpacking-the-design-pillars-of-a-chill-puzzle-game)
- Unpacking GDC 2022: no branching narrative; story inferred from objects; character biographies and a step-by-step plan of the protagonist's life drove item placement; placement limits communicate things about the unseen protagonist — [Game Developer GDC 2022 coverage](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/gdc-2022-unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items)
- A Short Hike (Adam Robinson-Yu, GDC 2020): started Dec 2018 as a side prototype while shelving a larger project; players explore freely, following trails or going off-path to find treasures — [80.lv GDC write-up](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-a-short-hike-postmortem); [Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/A_Short_Hike)
- Spiritfarer: Thunder Lotus said the concept came from wanting to discuss death "in a cozy and wholesome way"; GDC 2021 talk "Spiritfarer's Journey: Crafting Tenderness"; hugs between departing friends are a signature mechanic — [GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027409/-Spiritfarer-s-Journey-Crafting); [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/spiritfarer-review); [GamesSkinny interview](https://www.gameskinny.com/culture/thunder-lotus-on-spiritfarer-death-hugs-and-legacies/)
- Stardew Valley (Eric Barone): inspired by early Harvest Moon games; he avoids external deadlines and hype ("I prefer to work in an isolated bubble"); the relaxed pace is an interviewer's description, not a Barone design doctrine — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/simulation/eric-barone-isnt-worried-about-pressure-on-stardew-valley-follow-up-haunted-chocolatier-its-a-game-that-im-deciding-to-make-i-dont-need-to-make-it); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/01/24/g-s1-44510/the-legacy-and-future-of-the-farming-game-stardew-valley)
- Firewatch (Sean Vanaman, GDC 2014): court the player rather than expect suspension of disbelief; keep writing short with natural break points; writing should stay out of gameplay or be the gameplay; risk if few things to find in the world. GDC 2015 level-design talk (Anderson, Rodkin) was about meaningful exploration and choice without combat; GDC 2017 talk covered the dialogue system — [Game Developer on Vanaman](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/why-game-design-should-feel-like-taking-your-player-out-on-a-date); [GDC Vault 2015](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022108/contactUs); [GDC 2017 announcement](https://gdconf.com/news/come-gdc-2017-hear-firewatchs-unique-dialog-system-works)

### Inferences
- Failure handling: wrong item or word = a gentle redirect by a neighbour, no penalty, no score (matches Unpacking's no-fail design).
- Safety/abundance/softness: always-available basics (water, bread, a shaded bench), soft morning light, no scarcity pressure; the 08:00-11:00 clock is a gentle frame, not a timer.
- Pacing sessions: short complete errands (a few minutes) with a natural stopping point, resumable; 3 in-game hours gives a built-in arc without a streak.
- Kindness mechanics: help a neighbour, share, greet; reward is a warm reaction or a new door opening, not currency. Spiritfarer's "hug" suggests a simple, repeatable non-verbal kind act; adapt to all-male cast with a hand-to-chest greeting or handshake (design choice, not sourced).
- Environmental storytelling (Unpacking): tell the quarter's story via objects, labels and signs, which doubles as vocabulary context.
- Subtractive design: cut features until only contemplation/discovery/expression-like pillars remain; define 3 Arabic World pillars.

### Gaps
- No primary text found for Project Horseshoe's specific recommendations (report on projecthorseshoe.com not reached). 
- A Short Hike talk details on failure/pacing/NPC kindness not found; the full talk is on GDC Vault (gated).
- Firewatch pacing specifics and Stardew's own words on time pressure not found.
- Wholesome Games founding date and Direct viewership conflict/are single-sourced; treat as unverified.

## 5. Miyamoto / Nintendo on teaching through play; Animal Crossing's daily rhythm

### Takeaway
Miyamoto describes World 1-1 as a tutorial that lets players learn gradually and naturally by playing, no text; Animal Crossing ties to the real-time clock so play happens in short daily sessions.

### Cited Findings
- Miyamoto: World 1-1 contains everything a player needs to "gradually and naturally understand what they're doing"; once players grasp the core idea they move with more freedom and the game feels theirs — [Game Developer: How Miyamoto built World 1-1](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-miyamoto-built-i-super-mario-bros-i-legendary-world-1-1)
- Layout teaches without text: pipes of different heights teach that holding jump longer jumps higher; some pits have floors, so mistakes are survivable — [Wikipedia: World 1-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_1-1) (secondary); coverage of the Eurogamer interview: [MCV](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/video-miyamoto-shares-his-level-design-secrets/), [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/shigeru-miyamoto-explains-the-making-of-super-mario-bro-1798284041)
- Animal Crossing's real-time clock gives a daily rhythm; big changes (upgrading a house) take a real-world day or two so players play in short daily sessions; director Aya Kyogoku said the series is "tied to the real-time clock" and crafting/terraforming give players things to do off-hours — [Game Developer: New Horizons instant change](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/why-nintendo-s-new-i-animal-crossing-i-features-allow-instant-change-in-the-real-time-game)
- In Iwata Asks (New Leaf), the interviewer says time passing in sync with the real world gives a sense of unity and that fiddling with the clock undermines it — [Iwata Asks: New Leaf](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/3ds/animalcrossing-newleaf/0/1) (snippet; full page not fetched)

### Inferences
- Teach by arrangement: first errand in the quarter should make the core loop (tap object, see/hear Arabic word, bring it to someone) self-evident without a tutorial screen, with safe mistakes.
- Daily rhythm for Arabic World: a fixed in-game window (08:00-11:00) maps well to "one morning per visit"; consider a mild link to the player's real morning but do not pressure (no streaks); letting the clock advance in-game rather than by real time avoids punishing off-hours play.

### Gaps
- The Eurogamer/IGN primary Miyamoto interview text was not fetched, so level-element teaching points rest on secondary coverage. Iwata Asks pages beyond snippets not read; no direct quote on "daily rhythm" in Nintendo's own words confirmed.
