## A. Teaching method, ranked by agreement across all three channels

1. **Words and meaning first. Grammar comes late, light and by meaning** (A 19/20 · M 9/14 · U 7/16).
   - Sentence templates come first ("I want ___", "where is ___").
   - A grammar nudge appears only after the player has met a pattern in about 3 errands, or after a real mistake the player made.
   - Notes use plain names, not jargon.
   - The earliest point for grammar talk is about 500 words. The real test is whether the player can follow a scene in Arabic.
2. **Every word arrives inside a scene, never in a list.**
   - Only new words are tinted. The target is about 95% known words per line, matching Hu & Nation's 95–98%.
   - Progress shows as the tinted share shrinking, never as points.
   - Each word is met again in a second place in the city.
   - Support: A 17/20 · U 5/16.
3. **Try, then a kind recast, then try again. Mistakes are safe** (A 19/20 · M 9/14 · U 8/16).
   - The character repeats the player's fragment as a correct full sentence. "Water" becomes "I need water."
   - Only mistakes that change the meaning are corrected now. Style is left for later.
   - There is no fail screen.
   - A player who is confidently wrong gets a gentler, more concrete correction.
   - The first clean sentence is celebrated with a plain gesture: the item is handed over.
4. **A small daily amount, with review before anything new. Returning is always welcome** (M 10/14 · U 11/16 · A 9/20).
   - Each morning opens with 2–3 easy questions from yesterday plus one older one.
   - Only 1–3 new words each morning.
   - Each word is silently tracked as new, shaky or established, and shaky words come first.
   - A few minutes counts as a full visit. A returning player gets "welcome back", never a streak.
5. **Say it yourself, don't just pick it from options** (A 9/20 · U 5/16 · M 6/14).
   - The character asks and waits before giving any hint.
   - There is a cover-and-say step on shop signs.
   - The player builds a full sentence every few moves. A hidden internal count tracks how many, never shown as a score.
6. **Order things the way a child learns** (A 15/20 · M 5/14).
   - The order is: yourself, then your surroundings (room, lane, market, weather, directions), then your interests, then ideas.
   - Madinah adds a topic order: greetings, home, routine, food, work, shopping, weather, places, health.
   - Grammar follows the Madinah Reader order:
     - pointing ("this / that" by distance)
     - possession, which gets extra scenes
     - adjectives, singular first
     - verbs
7. **Mastery gate with one next step.**
   - The next errand opens when the player has used the words without help, not just finished the scene.
   - The screen always shows a single next errand. Learners quit when the next step is unclear.
   - Support: A 11/20 and 10/20 · U 7/16.
8. **Roots, each with a concrete picture first** (A 12/20 · U 8/16 · M 5/14).
   - Tapping a word opens a root card. It shows the physical image first, then 3–5 related words (k-t-b: book, office, library, writer).
   - Only everyday roots are used.
   - The notebook links each root to every scene where it came up.
9. **A pocket notebook** (A 13/20 · M 4/14 · U 6/16).
   - It fills only from errands, filed by place.
   - Nouns are shown with their plural. Verbs are shown as past, present and command, together with their particle.
   - It has a page of "words I wanted to say", which the next scene answers.
   - It has an "ask later" flag.
10. **A patient companion who speaks only Arabic** (M 8/14 · U 6/16).
    - He is one recurring kind male neighbour, with "again, please" and slow replay on every line.
    - Late in the game, the player teaches a word to a newer arrival, because teaching is the best review.
11. **Fusha first, dialect later and optional, and honest about the street** (A 16/20 · M 5/14).
    - A taxi driver laughs warmly that the hero "sounds like the news", then teaches the everyday word.
    - Karim's Egyptian-dialect lines become Fusha.
12. **The script**
    - Arabic script with audio is always on screen, and Latin letters are never the main text.
    - Full vowels are shown early. They fade word by word as each word is learned.
    - There is an optional mini-game with shop-sign letters, grouped by shape.
13. **Be honest about the plateau.**
    - Learners often quit after about 3–4 months, once errands work (the "four-month trap").
    - At that point the game adds a consolidation chapter and a calm "look how far you've come" moment, such as replaying the player's first sentence.
    - Arabic is presented as a strong foundation, not a promise of fluency.
14. **Short sessions** of about 10–15 minutes, untimed, with no notifications.

**Where the channels disagree, and our choice:**

| Question | The disagreement | Our choice |
|---|---|---|
| When grammar starts | 500 words vs 2,500 words | Use the "can you follow a scene" test |
| When to correct | Correct everything early vs only what blocks meaning | A light recast after the player's turn |
| How to repeat | Massed repetition vs spread over days | Spread over days (Cepeda 2006) |

## B. The hero's arrival story

The traveller's faith is never stated. Every scene is male-only and in the morning.

1. **Departure.** He leaves overconfident, with a big plan and a few stock phrases.
2. **Early-morning arrival at the port.** No one meets him. He overpays a cheerful taxi driver.
3. **One kind stranger.** An elder gives him dates and water. A porter leads him to the right lane.
4. **First errands.** Corner shop, bakery, laundry, bookstall, print shop, office counter. "Where are you from?"
5. **A comfort object.** His mug from home sits on the window sill.
6. **The lows.**
   - He freezes and can't say "paper" at the counter.
   - The office says "come back later".
   - The market overcharges him because he can't follow the numbers.
   - Day two is the lowest point.
7. **The neighbours' kindness.**
   - Bread is left at his door.
   - Someone notices he's absent and checks on him.
   - Someone offers him a lift without being asked.
8. **Practice.** He chooses Arabic over the easy route. He keeps his notebook, and he learns to say "again, please".
9. **The turning point.** One morning he understands a whole exchange at the bakery. The homesickness lifts.
10. **Giving back.** He helps a newer arrival with a bag and directions.

**Gentle conflicts:**
- wrong change, or haggling over a price
- "whose is this?"
- a lost key
- a cart blocking a doorway
- volume 2 missing from the bookstall
- "tea with or without milk?"
- water that only runs at a set hour

None of these are ever a danger.

## C. World and errands for the Hijazi port-city quarter

- **Places:**
  - bakery (bread, beans at the oven)
  - butcher
  - olive stall
  - grill
  - corner shop
  - laundry
  - print shop
  - bookstall with a dictionary shelf
  - pharmacy
  - taxi stand
  - office counter
  - engraver's shop for name plates
  - a free water-and-bread stop
  - a public well or fountain
  - a reading room
- **Scenes of everyday virtue that work for everyone:**
  - **The honest measure:** fill the container, add a handful on top, check the scale, hide no bad fruit.
  - **Keeping your word.**
  - **Hospitality:** dates and water at once for an unexpected guest. Locals give more directions than you asked for.
  - **Neighbourliness:** borrow salt, carry a plate next door.
  - **An apology that makes amends.**
  - **Shade and water** on a hot morning.
  - **Speech:** soft speech and no gossip.
- **Morning detail:**
  - shadows shortening through the window
  - reading the hour by shadow length
  - a quiet lull before the market fills
  - breakfast around 09:30
  - micro-lessons while bread bakes
- **Objects:** key, pen, book, lemon, sugar, milk, tea, coffee, dates, jug, bread, mug, scale, sack, reed pen, satchel, rope, water skin.
- **Nature words, each with its plural:** mountain, wind, stone, dust, valley, sea, rain.
- **"Thank you" replies:** a set of universal answers, from "you too" to "no trouble" to "it's nothing".
- **Map puzzles with place words:** in front, behind, here, there, first and second in a queue.

## E. Never use (from all readers)
- **Sacred material as lesson text:** Quran, hadith, fiqh, creed, du'a.
- **Prophets, Companions, scholars or study at the masjid** as content.
- **Any female person,** including masculine/feminine address drills. We teach masculine forms.
- **Points and streaks:**
  - points, badges, leaderboards, prizes, timed contests
  - the "don't miss two days" streak rule
- **Wrong tone:** fear or urgency framing, polemic, sects, war.
- **Wrong time or sound:** night, evening, Ramadan routines, melodic chants, music.
- **Bad text sources:** garbled auto-captions as Arabic data, and sales claims.
